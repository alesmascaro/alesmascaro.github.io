6  The fully Bayesian workflow

A fully Bayesian analysis uses one joint model for the graph, its admissible parameters and the observations. Let \(\mathfrak D_+=\{\mathcal D:d\in\operatorname{an}_{\mathcal D}(y)\}\) be the active graph class and let \(\mathfrak D_0\) contain structural-null graphs. For an active graph, the I-MAP map \(F_{\mathcal D}\) exposes \(\tau_{dy}=-\widetilde L_{dy}\). It leaves the Gaussian likelihood unchanged and restricts the transformed parameters to \(F_{\mathcal D}(\Theta_{\mathcal D})\).

The workflow consists of specifying graph and parameter priors, computing graph evidence, learning or sampling the graph, drawing its conditional parameter posterior, and averaging the resulting effects. The intended computational target is a graph score inexpensive enough for repeated evaluation, together with a sampler compatible with that same score and prior. The exact baseline in Chapter 4 and the normalized tilt below give tractable reference cases. General effect-centered priors need additional integration methods.

6.1 A common effect prior and the structural null

Let \(H=\mathbf1\{\mathcal D\in\mathfrak D_+\}\). An explicit specification is

\[ P(H=1)=\omega_H,\qquad p(\mathcal D)= \begin{cases} \omega_H p_+(\mathcal D),&\mathcal D\in\mathfrak D_+,\\ (1-\omega_H)p_0(\mathcal D),&\mathcal D\in\mathfrak D_0, \end{cases} \]

where each \(p_h\) is normalized on its graph class. This allows effect inclusion odds to be chosen separately from the number of graphs representing each class. Conditional on each active graph, the aim is the same effect marginal \(h(\tau)\), with a graph-specific nuisance distribution. The unconditional prior on the effect is then \((1-\omega_H)\delta_0+\omega_H h(\tau)\,d\tau\). This statement assumes continuous active-effect distributions; extra point masses require a corresponding explicit specification.

Attainable effects. In the unrestricted Gaussian DAG coefficient space, every active graph permits every real value of \(\tau_{dy}\).

NoteProof

To see this, choose one directed \(d\)-to-\(y\) path, assign its first coefficient the desired value \(t\), assign its other coefficients one, and set every other allowed coefficient to zero. The path sum is then \(t\), and any positive variances give a valid model.

This argument uses the allowed zero coefficients; additional sign, magnitude or faithfulness restrictions need their own support analysis.

An effect prior cannot be arbitrary outside the attainable effect support of a graph. Even with support \(\mathbb R\), the nuisance-coordinate domain may depend on \(\tau\). A normalized conditional specification

\[ \pi_{\mathcal D}(\tau,\eta)=h(\tau)\, q_{\mathcal D}(\eta\mid\tau),\qquad \int_{\mathcal N_{\mathcal D}(\tau)}q_{\mathcal D}(\eta\mid\tau)\,d\eta=1 \]

provides the requested effect marginal in a valid chart. A multi-chart construction needs consistent densities on overlapping domains.

Analytical construction by reweighting. Suppose a proper baseline prior has effect density \(g_{\mathcal D}(t)\), and the desired density \(h\) is absolutely continuous with respect to that effect distribution. Then, almost everywhere under the baseline,

\[ \pi_{1,\mathcal D}(\theta)= \frac{h\{\tau_{dy}(\theta)\}}{g_{\mathcal D}\{\tau_{dy}(\theta)\}} \pi_{0,\mathcal D}(\theta). \]

NoteProof

Integrating with respect to the baseline effect distribution gives \(\int h(t)\,dt=1\), and restricting the integral to an effect set gives exactly its probability under \(h\). The construction retains the baseline conditional distribution of nuisance quantities given the effect. It proves existence under domination without asserting a global independent-coordinate chart.

The practical obstacle is computing \(g_{\mathcal D}\) and controlling the ratio in its tails.

The chain in Section 2.3.1 supplies a simpler illustration. Draw \(\tau\sim h\), draw \(b\) from a proper continuous density with \(P(b=0)=0\), and draw positive variances from proper priors. Set \(c=\tau/b\). This defines a proper chain prior with the specified effect marginal. In original coordinates its density contains the factor \(|b|h(bc)\), multiplied by the nuisance density. The excluded chart boundary has zero probability under this construction, but can matter for numerical stability and for other priors. Locating an effect coefficient alone does not establish this normalization.

6.2 Priors on the constrained I-MAP space

Possible prior construction. Normal/inverse-gamma local priors can be assigned to selected free I-MAP entries, with the remaining entries determined by the model constraints, to influence \(\tau_{dy}=-\widetilde L_{dy}\). Such a specification, denoted “I-MAP-Wishart,” requires an admissible coordinate domain and a proper joint distribution; the label alone does not establish a conjugate family.

A prior on the full ambient I-MAP generally assigns probability zero to the lower-dimensional constrained image. Choosing a collection of “free” entries requires a valid chart, its domain, a rule for all branches of the constraints, and a reference measure. Independent priors in two different charts usually induce different priors on the same covariance model. The diamond chart dividing by \(b'\) already illustrates a domain problem (Section A.2).

For a smooth bijective chart transformation \(z=g(\theta)\), densities representing the same prior obey

\[ \pi_z(z)=\pi_\theta(g^{-1}(z))\left|\det Dg^{-1}(z)\right|. \]

For the embedded I-MAP image, work in coordinates of its actual dimension, not an ambient rectangular Jacobian determinant. A prior on the original DAG can always be pushed forward through \(F\); this preserves its marginal likelihood but does not independently choose an arbitrary marginal prior on the effect. Properness, effect support and the domains of a global or multi-chart nuisance specification must be established for each prior construction.

6.3 Laplace approximation and MLE invariance

Analytical identity. If a finite interior MLE exists, the constrained I-MAP estimate is \(F(\widehat\theta)\) by invariance under the smooth bijection onto the constrained image. It is not the unconstrained MLE of the denser I-MAP model.

For a proper smooth prior in \(k=q+|E|\) coordinates and a regular interior likelihood maximum,

\[ m(\mathbf X\mid\mathcal D)\approx e^{\ell(\widehat\theta)}\pi(\widehat\theta)(2\pi)^{k/2} |H(\widehat\theta)|^{-1/2}, \]

where \(H=-\nabla^2\ell\) is the observed information in those same coordinates. This MLE-centered expression is the leading approximation when the prior is smooth and positive nearby; a posterior-mode formulation incorporates prior curvature explicitly. Under standard fixed-dimensional conditions,

\[ \log m=\ell(\widehat\theta)-\tfrac{k}{2}\log n+O(1). \]

BIC discards prior- and graph-dependent constants, so it cannot retain all effects of an effect-centered prior. Changing coordinates for a fixed prior does not change the exact integral; the transformed density and curvature terms compensate in the leading Laplace expression. Using the number of ambient I-MAP entries over-penalizes the model. A non-local prior that vanishes at a null maximum needs a separate approximation; the displayed regular formula cannot simply be used there.

Exact nodewise integration after changing priors is available only if the full prior and likelihood still factor into independent blocks in valid coordinates. The fact that some equations are numerically unchanged does not by itself establish that condition. A split between exact integration and Laplace approximation requires a specified prior and a factorization proof.

6.4 Graph search

For any properly specified score \(m(\mathbf X\mid\mathcal D)\) and graph prior \(p(\mathcal D)\), the Metropolis-Hastings acceptance probability is

\[ \min\left\{1,\frac{m(\mathbf X\mid\mathcal D')p(\mathcal D')q(\mathcal D\mid\mathcal D')} {m(\mathbf X\mid\mathcal D)p(\mathcal D)q(\mathcal D'\mid\mathcal D)}\right\}. \]

Proposals can add, delete, or reverse edges, rejecting cycles. The skeleton-size prior \(p(\mathcal D)\propto\omega^{|E|}(1-\omega)^{q(q-1)/2-|E|}\) is a possible mass function on the chosen DAG space; orientation and DAG restrictions are part of that choice. Rejecting all moves that destroy \(d\in\operatorname{an}(y)\) targets a posterior conditional on ancestry. It cannot then estimate the probability of a structural null effect. A deterministic approximate marginal likelihood also targets an approximate posterior, not the exact one.

6.5 Conditional parameter sampling and model averaging

For a fixed graph, sampling can use original free coefficients and log variances, computing the I-MAP effect within each evaluation. This automatically respects the missing-edge constraints. A density defined in I-MAP chart coordinates must be converted to these original coordinates with its chart Jacobian; log-variance coordinates add the factor \(\prod_j v_j\). Alternatively, a valid I-MAP chart can be sampled directly. Hamiltonian or other Markov chain Monte Carlo methods require a specified target density, branch handling and convergence diagnostics. Sampling every ambient I-MAP coefficient freely changes the model.

If samples \(\theta^{(s)}\) come from a baseline posterior under \(\pi_0\), reweight by \(w_s\propto\pi_1(\theta^{(s)})/\pi_0(\theta^{(s)})\), provided both densities are in the same coordinates and reference measure. If they instead come from the baseline prior, include the likelihood as well. Jacobians are needed when comparing densities in different charts. Support coverage and finite weight moments matter; effective sample size and tail diagnostics assess the reliability of the weighted approximation.

For posterior draws \((\mathcal D^{(s)},\theta^{(s)})\), compute \(\tau^{(s)}=\tau_{dy}(\mathcal D^{(s)},\theta^{(s)})\), assigning zero to every structural-null draw. Averages and quantiles of these draws describe the model averaged effect posterior; its mean is

\[ E[\tau_{dy}\mid\mathbf X]=\sum_{\mathcal D}p(\mathcal D\mid\mathbf X) E[\tau_{dy}\mid\mathcal D,\mathbf X]. \]

Within-graph Monte Carlo weights and between-graph posterior masses must both refer to the same prior used in the scores. With reweighting \(r_{\mathcal D}=\pi_{1,\mathcal D}/\pi_{0,\mathcal D}\),

\[ m_1=m_0 E_0[r_{\mathcal D}\mid\mathbf X,\mathcal D],\qquad E_1[f\mid\mathbf X,\mathcal D]= \frac{E_0[f r_{\mathcal D}\mid\mathbf X,\mathcal D]} {E_0[r_{\mathcal D}\mid\mathbf X,\mathcal D]}. \]

These identities give a consistent route to evidence and conditional effect sampling. An estimated score used as if deterministic generally gives an approximate graph posterior. Exact pseudo-marginal sampling instead requires a nonnegative unbiased evidence estimator and an augmented-state acceptance rule that retains the current estimate; fresh random estimates in an ordinary acceptance ratio do not establish that target (Andrieu and Roberts 2009).

6.6 A normalized non-local alternative

Analytical result. Let a proper baseline prior on a fixed DAG be \(\pi_0(\theta\mid\mathcal D)\), with \(0<C_{\mathcal D}=E_0[\tau_{dy}^2\mid\mathcal D]<\infty\). Define

\[ \pi_M(\theta\mid\mathcal D)=\frac{\tau_{dy}(\theta)^2}{C_{\mathcal D}}\pi_0(\theta\mid\mathcal D). \]

The corresponding marginal likelihood satisfies

\[ m_M(\mathbf X\mid\mathcal D)=m_0(\mathbf X\mid\mathcal D) \frac{E_0[\tau_{dy}^2\mid\mathbf X,\mathcal D]}{C_{\mathcal D}}. \]

NoteProof

The baseline posterior density is \(p_0(\theta\mid\mathbf X,\mathcal D)=p(\mathbf X\mid\theta,\mathcal D)\pi_0(\theta\mid\mathcal D)/m_0(\mathbf X\mid\mathcal D)\). Consequently,

\[ m_M(\mathbf X\mid\mathcal D) =\frac1{C_{\mathcal D}}\int p(\mathbf X\mid\theta,\mathcal D)\tau_{dy}(\theta)^2\pi_0(\theta\mid\mathcal D)\,d\theta =\frac{m_0(\mathbf X\mid\mathcal D)}{C_{\mathcal D}}E_0[\tau_{dy}^2\mid\mathbf X,\mathcal D]. \]

This identity requires no I-MAP coordinates. The normalizing constant can be replaced by \(v\) only when the baseline effect marginal has second moment \(v\), for example \(\tau\sim N(0,v)\) in an admissible coordinate factorization. A general DAG-Wishart-induced path-sum prior need not have this second moment or even a finite one. DAGs with no \(d\)-to-\(y\) path have \(C_{\mathcal D}=0\); they require a separate null component rather than this tilt.

The tilted effect density is \(t^2g_{\mathcal D}(t)/C_{\mathcal D}\). It is therefore generally graph dependent. This tractable tilt illustrates effect-informed scoring, but does not itself impose a common moment-prior marginal across all active DAGs. For a specified common \(h\), the preceding \(h/g_{\mathcal D}\) construction is the relevant normalized reweighting.

The posterior second moment is exactly \(E_0[\tau\mid\mathbf X]^2+\operatorname{Var}_0(\tau\mid\mathbf X)\). A normal posterior approximation may replace these by \(\widehat\tau^2+\widehat V_\tau\), but \(\widehat V_\tau\) is not generally \(1/n\). For a regular parameter vector it is the delta-method quantity

\[ \widehat V_\tau=\nabla\tau(\widehat\theta)^\top H(\widehat\theta)^{-1}\nabla\tau(\widehat\theta). \]

For an unconstrained Gaussian regression on a valid \(Z\), the corresponding OLS variance coefficient is \(\sigma^2_{y\mid d,Z}/\{n\sigma^2_{d\mid Z}\}\). Approximation accuracy near path cancellation and in small samples depends on posterior shape, prior moments and the model-specific information matrix.

6.7 Computational organization

For each proposed DAG, obtain a topological ordering, identify its treatment-outcome mediator block and construct a closest ordering. Fit the original DAG’s node regressions, then apply the adjacent column transformations to their parameters. This gives the constrained I-MAP MLE without optimizing a denser model. Each dense column update costs \(O(q)\), so moving the outcome across \(|M|\) mediators costs \(O(q|M|)\) once an appropriate factorization is available. Constructing that initial factorization and the graph’s score has its own cost; this bound is not a benchmark for a complete graph search.

Effect-centered priors can couple several equations, so nodewise score caching needs a factorization argument. Cache graph structures, sufficient statistics and genuinely unaffected score blocks; update the changed prior normalization as well as the likelihood. The same prior density, effect map and evidence conventions should be called by graph scoring and posterior sampling. Modular functions make this consistency inspectable.

The appendix includes an executable three-graph benchmark (Section A.6): compatible DAG-Wishart scores are equal for the chain and its two equivalent orientations, while a properly normalized effect-moment tilt can change their posterior weights. For this example, its normalizing constants and evidence corrections are computed analytically. It validates those calculations, without asserting that a general I-MAP prior is conjugate or that an approximation is uniformly accurate.
