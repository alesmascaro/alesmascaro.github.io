5  The I-MAP construction and its constraints

5.1 Existence of a closest ordering

Assume the setting of Chapter 3 with \(d\in\operatorname{an}_{\mathcal D}(y)\). A closest ordering has the form

\[ \pi_{dy}=\langle\delta_1,d,M,y,\delta_3\rangle, \]

where the mediator block \(M\) is internally topological. Define \(S=\{d\}\cup M\cup\{y\}\) and \(A=\operatorname{an}_{\mathcal D}(S)\setminus S\). Choose a topological ordering within \(A\), then \(d,M,y\), then the remaining nodes. This is topological: every outside parent of \(S\) belongs to \(A\), and an ancestor of \(S\) descended from \(d\) must lie on a \(d\)-to-\(y\) path and so belongs to \(S\). No node in \(S\) can therefore be an ancestor of a node in \(A\). Since \(A\cup S\) is ancestral, all remaining nodes can be placed last. This establishes the existence of a closest ordering.

Move \(y\) just before the mediator block to obtain

\[ \pi^*=\langle\delta_1,d,y,M,\delta_3\rangle. \]

Different choices inside \(\delta_1,M,\delta_3\) may give different I-MAPs. “Optimal” here refers to the outcome regression’s adjustment efficiency; it does not identify a unique globally optimal graph.

5.2 Outcome parents and the effect coordinate

Proposition (outcome parents and effect coordinate). For any closest ordering, the graph-defined ordered minimal I-MAP \(G^*=G^{\pi^*}_{\mathcal D}\) has

\[ \operatorname{pa}_{G^*}(y)=\{d\}\cup O. \]

For every positive-variance parameter value of the original Gaussian DAG model, including unfaithful ones, its regression factorization in \(\pi^*\) satisfies

\[ -\widetilde L_{dy}=\tau_{dy}. \]

Proof. Every external parent of \(M\cup\{y\}\) is either \(d\) or lies in \(O\subseteq\delta_1\). Successive substitution of the equations for the mediators therefore gives

\[ X_y=\tau_{dy}X_d+\sum_{u\in O}c_uX_u+\eta, \]

where \(\eta\) is a linear combination of the errors at \(M\cup\{y\}\). Because the closest ordering is topological, \((X_{\delta_1},X_d)\) depends only on earlier errors, so \(\eta\) is independent of this entire predecessor vector. Its variance is positive because its coefficient on \(\epsilon_y\) is one. This is exactly the regression of \(X_y\) on \((X_{\delta_1},X_d)\); all predecessors outside \(\{d\}\cup O\) have zero coefficients.

For each \(u\in O\), an edge \(u\to c\), \(c\in M\cup\{y\}\), followed by a directed path from \(c\) to \(y\) supplies a monomial in \(c_u\). All internal nodes of this path lie in the mediator block. Distinct paths give distinct monomials with positive formal signs in the \(B\) coefficients; hence \(c_u\) is not the zero polynomial. Likewise, a directed \(d\)-to-\(y\) path makes \(\tau_{dy}\) a nonzero polynomial. These coefficients can be nonzero in the DAG model and thus their associated conditional independences are not graphically implied. By the ordered edge criterion, precisely these predecessor nodes are parents in \(G^*\). This proves the graph statement, while the substitution identity proves the effect equality at all parameter values. \(\square\)

Ancestors of \(d\) need not be separated from the outcome by conditioning on \(d\). For example, \(u\to d\), \(u\to y\) and \(d\to y\) leave \(u\) associated with \(y\) given \(d\). Such confounders are included in the external-parent regression.

5.3 Adjacent transposition

Let \(i,j\) be adjacent in the current factorization ordering, with \(i\) preceding \(j\), and put \(a=L_{ij}\). It is permissible that \(a=0\). Save the old variances \(v_i,v_j\) and set \(s=v_j+a^2v_i\). Apply these column operations in sequence:

\[ L_{\cdot j}^{E}=L_{\cdot j}-aL_{\cdot i},\qquad \widetilde L_{\cdot i}=L_{\cdot i}+\frac{av_i}{s}L_{\cdot j}^{E},\qquad \widetilde L_{\cdot j}=L_{\cdot j}^{E}, \]

and leave other columns unchanged. The new variances are

\[ \widetilde v_i=\frac{v_iv_j}{s},\qquad \widetilde v_j=s. \]

Swap \(i,j\) in the ordering. Both variance updates use the old values; an in-place update that reuses the new \(v_i\) in \(s\) is incorrect.

Proof of covariance preservation and DAG validity. The eliminated error is \(\epsilon_j^E=\epsilon_j-a\epsilon_i\), with variance \(s\) and covariance \(-av_i\) with \(\epsilon_i\). Replacing the latter by \(\widetilde\epsilon_i=\epsilon_i+(av_i/s)\epsilon_j^E\) makes these two errors uncorrelated, hence independent by Gaussianity, and gives variance \(v_iv_j/s\). The invertible equation operations leave \(X\) unchanged, so

\[ \widetilde L^{-\top}\widetilde D\widetilde L^{-1}=L^{-\top}DL^{-1}. \]

Adjacency ensures that both old columns involve only predecessors and the pair itself. After elimination the \(j\) column involves only earlier nodes; the \(i\) column involves those nodes and \(j\). The unit diagonal is retained and the new matrix is triangular in the swapped ordering. Its equations therefore constitute a DAG factorization. Uniqueness of Gaussian regressions on all predecessors identifies it with the modified Cholesky factorization in that ordering. \(\square\)

The covariance algebra alone is insufficient to establish a DAG for arbitrary \(i\to j\). In \(i\to k\to j\) with \(i\to j\), transposing \(i,j\) can introduce \(k\to i\) while retaining \(i\to k\). Adjacency is essential to DAG validity. Minimality follows from the ordered construction: for a faithful input distribution, numerical nonzero support gives the ordered minimal I-MAP; for a graph-defined output use d-separation in the original graph, rather than thresholding a particular parameter matrix.

5.4 Forward and reverse algorithms

Start with a closest ordering. Traverse \(M\) in reverse topological order and transpose each mediator with \(y\). At every step they are adjacent. If the current coefficient happens to be zero, swap their ordering with no numerical change. After the last step, the ordering is \(\pi^*\) and the parameters are its unique Gaussian regression factorization. Minimality relative to the original graph follows from the ordered d-separation criterion; it does not follow solely from a succession of locally minimal I-MAPs.

To reverse the construction, traverse \(M\) in its original topological order, transposing \(y\) with each mediator. Each transposition is an involution on its adjacent factorization pair: alternatively, uniqueness in the restored ordering proves that the reverse sequence recovers the original \((L,D)\) on the constrained image. Applied to an unconstrained Gaussian factorization on \(G^*\), it recovers a factorization in \(\pi_{dy}\) that can have extra coefficients.

With exactly one mediator, a single adjacent transposition in a closest ordering produces the effect coefficient. The existence of an edge \(m\to y\) alone does not imply that \(m\) is the sole mediator.

5.5 Exact characterization of the constrained image

Let \(\Theta_{\mathcal D}\) have one free coefficient per allowed edge and \(q\) positive variances. Let \(\Theta_{G^*}\) be the analogous ambient space. The change of ordering \(F\) maps the former into the latter while preserving \(\Sigma\).

For \(\widetilde\theta\in\Theta_{G^*}\), reconstruct its covariance and factorize it in the original ordering, obtaining \((L^{\mathrm{back}},D^{\mathrm{back}})\). Then

\[ F(\Theta_{\mathcal D})= \left\{\widetilde\theta\in\Theta_{G^*}: L^{\mathrm{back}}_{uv}=0\ \text{for every }u\prec_{\pi_{dy}}v\text{ with }u\to v\notin E\right\}. \]

Proof. Necessity follows from reversing \(F\). For sufficiency, the imposed zeros place the restored factorization in \(\Theta_{\mathcal D}\). Refactorizing it in \(\pi^*\) returns the unique original \(\widetilde\theta\). All denominators are positive conditional variances. Consequently \(F\) and its inverse are smooth on the positive-variance domains, and the image has dimension \(q+|E|\), even though the ambient I-MAP has dimension \(q+|E(G^*)|\). \(\square\)

This characterization avoids any need to infer constraints from a single numerical sparsity pattern. Constraints may be redundant; their number need not equal their rank. A choice of which entries to solve for can fail at zero denominators, so a global free-coordinate chart and variation independence of the effect from the nuisance parameters require additional conditions.

The construction preserves the observational model. The alternative arrows generally do not retain the original intervention semantics. The distinguished effect coefficient is justified by the preceding theorem; other intervention effects must still be evaluated from the original causal model.
