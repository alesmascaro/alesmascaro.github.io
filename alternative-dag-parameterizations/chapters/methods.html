4  Preliminaries

4.1 Bayesian causal inference with an unknown DAG

A conventional Bayesian analysis places a mass function \(p(\mathcal D)\) on a specified DAG space \(\mathfrak D\) and a parameter prior \(\pi_{0,\mathcal D}(\theta)\) on each graph’s Gaussian model. The observational likelihood is

\[ p(\mathbf X\mid\theta,\mathcal D) =(2\pi)^{-nq/2}\prod_{j=1}^q v_j^{-n/2} \exp\left\{-\frac12\sum_{j=1}^q\frac{1}{v_j}\sum_{i=1}^n \left(x_{ij}+\sum_{u\in P_j}L_{uj}x_{iu}\right)^2\right\}. \]

Here means are known to be zero, as in Chapter 3. The marginal likelihood integrates over the free coefficients and positive variances:

\[ m_0(\mathbf X\mid\mathcal D)=\int_{\Theta_{\mathcal D}} p(\mathbf X\mid\theta,\mathcal D)\pi_{0,\mathcal D}(\theta)\,d\theta. \]

Bayes’ rule yields

\[ p_0(\mathcal D\mid\mathbf X)= \frac{m_0(\mathbf X\mid\mathcal D)p(\mathcal D)} {\sum_{\mathcal D'\in\mathfrak D}m_0(\mathbf X\mid\mathcal D')p(\mathcal D')}. \]

For a small graph space the sum can be evaluated directly. Larger spaces usually require a search or a graph sampler, using scores and proposal probabilities rather than enumerating every DAG. Conditional on a sampled DAG, draw its parameters from the corresponding posterior and compute \(\tau_{dy}(\mathcal D,\theta)\). This is the basic graph-and-parameter averaging architecture of Bayesian effect inference (Castelletti and Consonni 2021; Castelletti and Mascaro 2021; Pensar et al. 2020).

4.2 From parameter posteriors to an effect posterior

For any measurable effect set \(A\),

\[ P_0(\tau_{dy}\in A\mid\mathbf X)= \sum_{\mathcal D\in\mathfrak D}p_0(\mathcal D\mid\mathbf X) \int_{\Theta_{\mathcal D}}\mathbf1\{\tau_{dy}(\mathcal D,\theta)\in A\} \pi_{0,\mathcal D}(\theta\mid\mathbf X)\,d\theta. \]

The same operation induces the prior effect distribution before observing data. In ordinary edge coordinates, the active effect is a path-sum polynomial. Its induced distribution depends on the graph, its number and arrangement of paths, and its coefficient prior. Even identical local coefficient priors can therefore give different effect distributions in different DAGs.

Graphs without a directed \(d\)-to-\(y\) path contribute a point mass at zero. Under continuous coefficient priors, path cancellation in an ancestor graph has probability zero, although the parameter space includes such values. Thus a typical averaged effect posterior combines a structural-null atom with continuous active-effect components. Graph uncertainty persists within a Markov equivalence class unless prior or additional causal information distinguishes its members.

4.3 Exact DAG-Wishart baseline

The DAG-Wishart distribution is a conjugate prior for Gaussian DAG models (Ben-David et al. 2015; Peluso and Consonni 2020). In the \(L=I-B\) convention, its normalization follows by completing the square. For centered independent observations collected in an \(n\times q\) matrix \(\mathbf X\), take \(U\succ0\) and shape vector \(\alpha\) with \(\alpha_j>|P_j|+2\), \(P_j=\operatorname{pa}_{\mathcal D}(j)\). The prior density with respect to the free entries of \(L\) and the variances is

\[ \pi(L,D\mid\mathcal D)=\frac1{Z_{\mathcal D}(\alpha,U)} \exp\{-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(LD^{-1}L^\top U)\} \prod_j v_j^{-\alpha_j/2}. \]

Put \(p_j=|P_j|\), \(K_j=U_{P_jP_j}\), \(u_j=U_{P_jj}\), \(s_j=U_{jj}-u_j^\top K_j^{-1}u_j\), and \(r_j=(\alpha_j-p_j)/2-1\). Empty parent sets have determinant one and \(s_j=U_{jj}\). Completing the square in the exponent and then integrating the variance gives

\[ Z_{\mathcal D}(\alpha,U)=\prod_j (2\pi)^{p_j/2}|K_j|^{-1/2}\Gamma(r_j)(s_j/2)^{-r_j}. \]

NoteProof

Indeed the coefficient integral contributes \((2\pi v_j)^{p_j/2}|K_j|^{-1/2}\), and the remaining integral is that of an inverse-gamma kernel.

Equivalently, independently over nodes,

\[ v_j\sim\operatorname{IG}(r_j,s_j/2),\qquad L_{P_jj}\mid v_j\sim N(-K_j^{-1}u_j,v_jK_j^{-1}). \]

Here \(\operatorname{IG}(r,b)\) has density \(b^r\Gamma(r)^{-1}v^{-r-1}e^{-b/v}\). Marginally the coefficient vector is Student \(t\) with \(2r_j\) degrees of freedom, center \(-K_j^{-1}u_j\), and scale matrix \(s_jK_j^{-1}/(2r_j)\); that scale is not its covariance matrix.

The posterior updates are \(\alpha_j\mapsto\alpha_j+n\) and \(U\mapsto U+\mathbf X^\top\mathbf X\). Thus

\[ m_0(\mathbf X\mid\mathcal D)=(2\pi)^{-nq/2} \frac{Z_{\mathcal D}(\alpha+n\mathbf1,U+\mathbf X^\top\mathbf X)}{Z_{\mathcal D}(\alpha,U)}. \]

A compatible choice is \(\alpha_j=a-q+2p_j+3\) with \(a>q-1\) and common \(U\), yielding the score-equivalent construction discussed by Peluso and Consonni. The scalar \(a\) is a shape hyperparameter; \(U\) is the scale/rate matrix in the exponential kernel. Arbitrary DAG-Wishart shapes need not yield equal marginal likelihoods for Markov-equivalent graphs.

4.4 Compatibility and what it controls

With compatible parameter priors, equivalent observational models have equal integrated scores. This provides a controlled baseline for graph comparison. It does not prescribe equal prior mass for active and null causal-effect hypotheses, and it does not directly specify the active-effect marginal. A prior informed by causal knowledge can intentionally depart from score equivalence, provided its meaning and normalization are clear.

The I-MAP workflow retains the same likelihood and averaging architecture. Its departure lies in how the prior is organized around the causal effect, and in the integration and sampling needed for that prior. The exact conjugate score above supplies a benchmark for those computations; reparameterizing a fixed prior must reproduce it.
