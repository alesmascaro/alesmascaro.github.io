3  Notation and context

The graph describes the causal system, while a variable ordering describes an observational regression factorization. Keeping these objects distinct is essential: changing the ordering can expose a causal effect without giving every new arrow a causal interpretation.

Symbol Meaning
\(\mathcal D=(V,E)\) Original causal DAG; \(q=|V|\)
\(X\), \(\mathbf X\) Random vector and \(n\times q\) observation matrix
\(B_{uj}\), \(L_{uj}=-B_{uj}\) Structural coefficient for \(u\to j\) and its modified Cholesky entry, for \(u\ne j\)
\(D=\operatorname{diag}(v_1,\ldots,v_q)\) Positive structural error variances
\(d,y\), \(\tau_{dy}\) Treatment, outcome and total causal effect
\(M\), \(O\), \(Z\) Mediators, O-set and a candidate adjustment set
\(\pi\), \(G^\pi_{\mathcal D}\) Variable ordering and its graph-defined ordered I-MAP
\(\widetilde L,\widetilde D\) Parameters after changing the observational ordering
\(\theta_{\mathcal D}\), \(\Theta_{\mathcal D}\) Free original-DAG parameters and their domain
\(\pi_{\mathcal D}(\theta)\), \(p(\mathcal D)\), \(m(\mathbf X\mid\mathcal D)\) Parameter-prior density, graph-prior mass and marginal likelihood

The symbol \(\pi\) without a subscript denotes an ordering; \(\pi_{\mathcal D}(\theta)\) denotes a density. Parent sets are \(P_j=\operatorname{pa}_{\mathcal D}(j)\), whereas all predecessors in an ordering are \(R_j^\pi\). Matrices retain the original node labels unless a permutation is stated explicitly.

3.1 Graphs and Gaussian models

Let \(\mathcal D=(V,E)\) be a DAG on \(V=\{1,\ldots,q\}\). Write \(\operatorname{pa}_{\mathcal D}(j)\), \(\operatorname{an}_{\mathcal D}(j)\) and \(\operatorname{de}_{\mathcal D}(j)\) for parents, strict ancestors and strict descendants. These sets exclude the node itself. An ordering is topological when every parent precedes its children. For a set, parents and ancestors denote unions of the corresponding node sets.

Use \(\mathcal D\) for the graph and \(D=\operatorname{diag}(v_1,\ldots,v_q)\) for positive error variances. The centered structural equations are

\[ X=B^\top X+\epsilon,\qquad \epsilon\sim N_q(0,D),\qquad L=I-B, \]

where \(B_{uj}=0\) if \(u\notin\operatorname{pa}_{\mathcal D}(j)\). Allowed edge coefficients may equal zero; requiring every allowed coefficient to be nonzero would unnecessarily exclude legitimate parameter values and would still not guarantee faithfulness. Consequently,

\[ L^\top X=\epsilon,\qquad \Sigma=L^{-\top}DL^{-1},\qquad \Omega=LD^{-1}L^\top. \]

All errors are mutually independent. There are no hidden variables, selection effects, or feedback loops. Means are assumed known to be zero in the likelihood calculations. Estimating an intercept or mean requires changing those calculations and their parameter counts.

3.2 Interventions and total effects

A hard intervention \(\operatorname{do}(X_d=x)\) replaces the equation for \(X_d\) by the constant \(x\) and removes its incoming edges. The remaining structural equations are retained. For \(d\ne y\), define

\[ \tau_{dy}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}E[X_y\mid\operatorname{do}(X_d=x)] =\sum_{p:d\rightsquigarrow y}\prod_{(u\to v)\in p} B_{uv} =[L^{-1}]_{dy}. \]

NoteProof

Since \(B\) is nilpotent after topological permutation, \((I-B)^{-1}=I+B+\cdots+B^{q-1}\). For distinct endpoints its \((d,y)\) entry enumerates the directed paths. Replacing the treatment equation yields the same propagation along paths starting at \(d\).

If no directed path exists, \(\tau_{dy}=0\) for all parameters. If a path exists, opposing path contributions may still cancel. Thus ancestry and a nonzero effect are different statements at particular parameter values. For simultaneous interventions, paths through another intervened treatment are removed; the single-treatment formula cannot be reused without this change. Joint interventions require a separate construction (Nandy et al. 2017; Castelletti and Mascaro 2021).

3.3 Adjustment and the O-set

A set \(Z\subseteq V\setminus\{d,y\}\) is valid for adjustment if, for every causal distribution compatible with the original DAG,

\[ p(x_y\mid\operatorname{do}(X_d=x)) =\int p(x_y\mid X_d=x,X_Z=z)p(z)\,dz. \]

For \(Z=\varnothing\), the right side is \(p(x_y\mid X_d=x)\). In a Gaussian model write the observational conditional mean as

\[ E[X_y\mid X_d,X_Z]=\beta_d X_d+\beta_Z^\top X_Z. \]

If \(Z\) is valid, integrating this expression proves \(\beta_d=\tau_{dy}\). The adjustment identity does not generally equate the observational conditional law with \(p(x_y\mid\operatorname{do}(X_d=x),X_Z=z)\) for an arbitrary valid set.

Suppose \(d\in\operatorname{an}_{\mathcal D}(y)\) and let

\[ M=\operatorname{de}_{\mathcal D}(d)\cap\operatorname{an}_{\mathcal D}(y),\qquad C=M\cup\{y\},\qquad O=\operatorname{pa}_{\mathcal D}(C)\setminus(\{d\}\cup M). \]

The general O-set definition removes the treatment and the causal nodes together with their descendants. In this single-treatment DAG setting, an external parent of \(C\) cannot be a descendant of \(C\) without a directed cycle, so it reduces to the displayed expression. An external parent descended from \(d\) would itself lie in \(M\); hence \(O\) contains only non-descendants of \(d\).

Source-supported result. The O-set is valid and minimizes the asymptotic variance of the OLS total-effect estimator over valid adjustment sets under the causal linear-model assumptions (Henckel et al. 2022; Witte et al. 2020). This is not a claim of universal finite-sample or Bayesian optimality. With \(A=(d,O)\) in that order,

\[ \tau_{dy}=[\Sigma_{AA}^{-1}\Sigma_{Ay}]_1,\qquad \widehat\tau_{dy}=[S_{AA}^{-1}S_{Ay}]_1. \]

\(S\) is the sample covariance matrix (centered when fitting an intercept). Equivalently, use \((W^\top W)^{-1}W^\top x_y\) for the regression design \(W\). The asymptotic variance is

\[ \operatorname{avar}(\widehat\tau_{dy})= \frac{\operatorname{Var}(X_y\mid X_d,X_O)}{n\operatorname{Var}(X_d\mid X_O)}. \]

3.4 Independence maps and ordered regressions

Let \(\mathcal I(\mathcal D)\) denote all graphical d-separations, and \(\mathcal I(p)\) all distributional conditional independences. A DAG \(G\) is an I-MAP of \(p\) if \(\mathcal I(G)\subseteq\mathcal I(p)\), and an I-MAP of \(\mathcal D\) if \(\mathcal I(G)\subseteq\mathcal I(\mathcal D)\). This latter definition does not require choosing a faithful distribution. Faithfulness means \(\mathcal I(p)=\mathcal I(\mathcal D)\).

For ordering \(\pi\), put \(R_{\pi_j}^\pi=\{\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_{j-1}\}\), the predecessors of the node \(\pi_j\). Define the ordered graph by

\[ \pi_i\to\pi_j\in G^\pi_{\mathcal D} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad i<j\ \text{and}\ \pi_i\not\!\perp_{\mathcal D}\pi_j\mid R_{\pi_j}^\pi\setminus\{\pi_i\}. \]

The edge criterion uses dependence. For nonsingular Gaussians, the corresponding distribution-specific construction keeps exactly the nonzero coefficients in each regression on all predecessors. Gaussian conditional independence satisfies the intersection property, so removing all zero coefficients leaves a valid factorization. Each retained coefficient is necessary: deleting it would impose a conditional independence that fails in that regression. Thus the distribution-specific graph is a minimal I-MAP. Taking a faithful Gaussian distribution of \(\mathcal D\) gives the graphical ordered minimal I-MAP above; its definition applies to the entire DAG model.

Minimal means no edge can be removed while retaining the I-MAP property; it does not mean globally fewest edges. At unfaithful parameter values, a regression coefficient can vanish on an edge of the graph-defined I-MAP. Keep the graph and its allowed coefficients separate.
