8  Discussion, conclusions and open questions

8.1 Interpretation

The construction provides a precise outcome regression coordinate for the total effect while retaining every observed variable. Its extra arrows enlarge the unconstrained Gaussian model; the restored missing-edge equations recover the original sparse model. The target coefficient identity holds even at path cancellations, while the graph-defined parent set does not change with individual coefficient values.

This distinction explains both the appeal and the difficulty of Bayesian inference. The effect is easily located, but independent priors on arbitrary subsets of ambient entries do not automatically define a coherent prior on the original model. Chart choices, domains, normalization and Jacobians can matter. A pushforward of a fixed prior preserves Bayesian evidence; specifying new independent chart priors changes the scientific model.

8.2 Scope and limitations

The results concern one treatment and one outcome, Gaussian observational factorizations, positive error variances, independent errors, and a causal original DAG. They do not identify a causal DAG from observational data, extend automatically to hidden confounding or joint interventions, or preserve all intervention interpretations of the alternative arrows.

The O-set’s efficiency guarantee is asymptotic and frequentist. The Bayesian adjustment decision depends on its specified reference posterior and loss. Neither implies the other. BIC may approximate evidence under regularity, but discards prior constants; it cannot establish improved effect-prior calibration. Near null effects, non-local priors require appropriate normalization and approximations.

8.3 Comparison with frugal parameterization

Both constructions expose causal quantities, but the I-MAP result here focuses on a single mean derivative within a full-variable Gaussian factorization. Frugal parameterization directly organizes a causal distribution and its nuisance pieces under explicit smoothness and compatibility assumptions (Evans and Didelez 2024). Sparse-DAG comparisons require explicit constraints on the common observational model.

The chain in Section 2.3.1 remains non-redundant, but its displayed frugal blocks lose variation independence. The proof in Section 2.3.2 gives the corresponding I-MAP constraint and the consequences for independent ambient priors. Both require admissible nuisance domains, normalization and Jacobians. This shared difficulty does not imply that every sparse DAG loses variation independence or that prescribing an effect prior is impossible: other charts and conditional priors can work. Neither improved marginal-likelihood accuracy nor a complete graph-learning workflow follows solely from exposing a causal coordinate. Those properties depend on the constrained model, prior and inference method. The forbidden-projection approach also remains relevant under graph uncertainty: one can compute a projection within each candidate DAG. Marginalizing variables changes the inferential construction, but does not make that approach impossible (Witte et al. 2020).

8.5 Conclusions

A total causal effect can be represented as a coefficient in an ordered Gaussian I-MAP, with outcome adjustment by the O-set. Adjacent transpositions compute this factorization, and reverse-order missing-edge constraints preserve the original DAG model. The observational reparameterization does not in general preserve the causal interpretation of every alternative arrow.

Bayesian inference requires more than locating the effect coefficient. The prior must be proper on admissible coordinates of the constrained model, and its density must transform with the appropriate Jacobian. A normalized second-moment tilt yields an exact evidence identity, while adjustment decisions depend on the specified posterior and loss.

8.6 Open questions

Effect-centered priors (Q004). When are nuisance coordinates globally variation independent of the effect? How can multi-chart or conditional priors accommodate path cancellation and zero-coordinate boundaries while retaining tractable normalization? How does the attainable effect support change under additional coefficient restrictions?

Evidence approximation (Q004). Under what conditions do Laplace approximations and posterior second-moment approximations remain accurate near null effects? How do prior sensitivity and importance-weight tails affect graph comparison?

Joint computation (Q004). Which admissible prior families support graph scores that can be repeatedly evaluated at useful cost and conditional samplers targeting those same priors? Can effect-informed scoring retain enough factorization for efficient updates without changing the intended sparse model?

Adjustment decisions (Q005). Which posterior loss captures the desired balance between point-estimation error, regression uncertainty and adjustment validity under graph uncertainty? What graphical or computational characterization follows from that loss?

Two-step uncertainty (Q005). How should a modular procedure report uncertainty after graph learning and adjustment selection? What calibration and sensitivity properties follow when the steps share an effect prior but use different nuisance models?

Parameterization scope (Q006). How can the explicit sparse-model constraints in I-MAP and frugal coordinates be used efficiently across graph search, and how do they compare with forbidden projection? Which parts of the construction extend to joint interventions or hidden confounding?
