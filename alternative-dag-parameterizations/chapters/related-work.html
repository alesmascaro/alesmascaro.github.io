2  Related work

2.1 Graphs, interventions, and uncertainty

Pearl’s graphical framework provides the language of conditional independence and interventions (Pearl 1988, 2009). IDA estimates possible effects across DAGs compatible with an observational equivalence class (Maathuis et al. 2009). Joint interventions require additional methods (Nandy et al. 2017), and ancestral-graph approaches allow latent-variable uncertainty under their own assumptions (Malinsky and Spirtes 2016). The generalized adjustment criterion covers DAGs and several classes of partially identified graphs (Perković et al. 2018). These results motivate graph uncertainty without making an individual causal DAG observationally identifiable.

Henckel, Perković and Maathuis characterize the asymptotically efficient O-set for linear-model adjustment (Henckel et al. 2022). Witte and colleagues give its forbidden-projection interpretation and integrate it into effect estimation under graph uncertainty (Witte et al. 2020). The I-MAP construction here retains all nodes and uses a constrained reordering; forbidden projection marginalizes selected nodes. They are related constructions with different inferential roles.

2.2 Bayesian graph and effect inference

DAG-Wishart priors supply a conjugate baseline (Ben-David et al. 2015). Compatible priors for model comparison impose additional shape restrictions (Peluso and Consonni 2020), connected to the parameter-prior construction framework of Geiger and Heckerman (Geiger and Heckerman 2002). Conjugacy alone does not imply score equivalence.

Bayesian effect inference can average over graph and parameter uncertainty in Gaussian systems (Castelletti and Consonni 2021; Pensar et al. 2020), including simultaneous interventions (Castelletti and Mascaro 2021). Categorical-model extensions have also been developed (Castelletti et al. 2024).

2.3 Frugal parameterization

Evans and Didelez directly parameterize a causal distribution and build the remaining model around it (Evans and Didelez 2024). The I-MAP target here is a mean derivative in a constrained Gaussian DAG representation. Comparisons of redundancy or inferential accuracy require explicit sparse-model constraints and a common inferential target. See Q006 and the discussion.
