8  Adjustment decisions under graph uncertainty

8.1 A well-defined action space

An adjustment regression can be selected by minimizing posterior expected loss. Let \(\tau=\tau_{dy}(\mathcal D,\theta)\) have a proper reference posterior \(p(\mathcal D,\theta\mid\mathbf X)\) with a finite second moment. For each candidate \(Z\), specify a regression prior and obtain a reported number \(a_Z(\mathbf X)\), such as its posterior mean. This action is computable from the data and \(Z\); it cannot depend on an unknown true DAG unless a further, observable rule averages those DAG-specific actions.

“Bayesian OLS” does not by itself specify a posterior. A regression likelihood, prior, and treatment of the residual variance are required. A regression coefficient for an invalid set is an associational estimand, even if it is assigned a Bayesian posterior. The reference posterior for the causal effect and the candidate regression posteriors serve different roles.

8.2 Squared-loss decision rule

Take \(L(Z,\tau)=(a_Z-\tau)^2\). Put \(\mu=E[\tau\mid\mathbf X]\) and \(V=\operatorname{Var}(\tau\mid\mathbf X)\). Expanding around \(\mu\) gives the exact risk

\[ R(Z\mid\mathbf X)=(a_Z-\mu)^2+V, \qquad Z^*\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_Z(a_Z-\mu)^2. \]

Analytical result. The chosen action is closest to the Bayesian model averaged effect mean among the available actions. The posterior variance of the reference effect is constant across \(Z\). The regression posterior variance \(s_Z^2\) does not appear. Thus squared loss for the reported point action does not separately penalize the candidate estimator’s variance. The full Bayes risk is not equal to its first term; that term is only an equivalent minimization objective.

With \(\mu_{\mathcal D}=E[\tau\mid\mathcal D,\mathbf X]\) and \(V_{\mathcal D}=\operatorname{Var}(\tau\mid\mathcal D,\mathbf X)\), the law of total variance gives

\[ V=\sum_{\mathcal D}p(\mathcal D\mid\mathbf X)V_{\mathcal D} +\operatorname{Var}_{\mathcal D\mid\mathbf X}(\mu_{\mathcal D}). \]

If one formally writes a DAG-dependent action \(a_{\mathcal D,Z}\), its risk is

\[ \sum_{\mathcal D}p(\mathcal D\mid\mathbf X) \{(a_{\mathcal D,Z}-\mu_{\mathcal D})^2+V_{\mathcal D}\}. \]

The constant term here is the within-DAG variance; the total mixture variance additionally includes between-DAG variation. This expression describes an action that observes \(\mathcal D\); it is not an implementable decision under graph uncertainty without an additional rule. If \(a_{\mathcal D,Z}=a_Z\) for every DAG, minimizing the first term recovers the preceding closest-to-\(\mu\) rule, with the between-DAG constant retained in the full risk.

8.3 Validity, precision, and alternative losses

Proposals. Distinct objectives need distinct specifications. Possible choices include:

  • Restrict to sets valid in every DAG with posterior support, if such sets exist; that intersection can be empty.
  • Require \(P(Z\text{ is valid in }\mathcal D\mid\mathbf X)\ge1-\varepsilon\), documenting the chosen tolerance and the posterior graph model.
  • Use the decision criterion \((a_Z-\mu)^2+\lambda s_Z^2\) with a specified \(\lambda\ge0\) to penalize regression posterior dispersion. This is a new criterion, not a consequence of the original squared-loss rule.
  • Evaluate predictive or repeated-sampling risk with an explicit data-generating model if the goal is estimator bias and sampling variance.

Enumeration of candidates is one computational approach, not a demonstrated necessary or uniquely available algorithm. The union of graph-specific valid sets contains sets invalid in other supported DAGs. A graphical characterization of a Bayesian criterion depends on its particular loss and posterior specification. The frequentist O-set result optimizes a different quantity (Chapter 3).

8.4 Structural null effects and shrinkage estimators

Consider the shrinkage rule \(\tau^{PB}=P(\tau\ne0\mid\mathbf X)\,\tau^B\). Let \(H\) indicate an active effect model, with \(\tau=0\) under \(H=0\). Then

\[ E[\tau\mid\mathbf X]=P(H=1\mid\mathbf X)E[\tau\mid H=1,\mathbf X]. \]

This is a coherent squared-loss estimator if \(\tau^B\) is the conditional active-model posterior mean. If \(\tau^B\) is already the unconditional model averaged mean, multiplying by \(P(H=1\mid\mathbf X)\) applies the inclusion factor twice. If \(\tau^B\) is selected from adjustment regressions, the product needs its own loss-based justification.

Under absolutely continuous priors on original DAG coefficients, a nonzero path-sum polynomial equals zero on a Lebesgue-null set. Therefore, within an ancestor DAG, exact cancellation has prior and posterior probability zero whenever the posterior remains absolutely continuous; DAGs without ancestry supply a point mass at zero. This almost-sure relation does not say every ancestor parameter value has a nonzero effect. If graph sampling is restricted to ancestry, that structural-null uncertainty has already been removed (Chapter 7).
